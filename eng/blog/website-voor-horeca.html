Website for hospitality businesses: how guests book a table faster

24 August 2026 6 min read

A hospitality guest decides within seconds. Someone searching for "restaurant nearby" or "cafe open now" is often already outside or on the way, and wants to know immediately: is it open, what does it cost, and can I get a table right now? A website that doesn't show this at a glance loses the guest to the next search result.

Hospitality is one of the few industries where the website is almost always checked on a phone, often at the last moment. That calls for different priorities than an average business website. Below are the points that make the difference.

1. Always up-to-date, directly readable menu

The biggest frustration with hospitality websites: a six-month-old PDF menu that's barely readable on a phone and no longer matches the prices. Put the menu on the site as regular, searchable text (not a separate PDF file), so it's easy to keep current and Google can actually read and show the dishes in search results.

2. Book in one click

"Call us to book" is a barrier. Offer at least a direct call button, a WhatsApp button, and, where possible, an online booking link. The fewer steps between "I want to eat here" and a confirmed table, the fewer guests drop off.

3. Opening hours that are actually correct, including holidays

Few things are more annoying than showing up to a closed door because the website still shows old hours. State current opening hours prominently, including different hours around holidays, and keep them structurally updated.

4. Show real photos of dishes and interior

Stock photos of random pasta dishes convince no one. Photos of your own dishes, your bar, your terrace: that's what a guest bases their choice on between you and the place down the street.

5. Address and directions directly clickable

A hospitality guest is often on the move. Make sure the address links directly to Google Maps, and clearly mention parking options or the nearest public transport stop.

6. Reviews visible on the site itself

Your Google rating is valuable, but guests only check it after clicking to your website. Show a few strong reviews directly on the homepage instead of only pointing to Google.

7. Allergy and dietary information easy to find

More and more guests filter for gluten-free, vegetarian, or vegan before booking. Make this explicitly findable in the menu instead of only finding out on the spot what is and isn't possible.

8. Blazing fast on mobile

Because virtually all hospitality traffic is mobile, and often on the move with patchy signal, load speed isn't a nice-to-have but a direct requirement. A website that loads slowly on 4G costs you guests before they've even seen the menu.

What a hospitality website should NOT do

  • An outdated or unreadable PDF menu
  • Outdated opening hours
  • No direct way to book
  • An address that doesn't link to a map
  • Slow load times on mobile
